Think It Over: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 November 2026

Think It Over is made by Support Turtles, an independent developer in British Columbia, Canada. This page explains, in plain words, what the app does with information about you. If anything here is unclear, write to supportturtles@duck.com and we will answer.

The short version

  • There are no accounts. We never ask for your name, email address, phone number or location.
  • There are no ads, no advertising IDs and no third-party analytics tools in the app. We do not sell information about you, and we do not track you across other apps or websites.
  • Reading questions needs nothing from you and happens on your phone. Your past conversations are saved on your phone, not on our server.
  • When the app opens, it asks our server how many conversations you have left today. Nothing from that check is stored.
  • When you talk a question through, what you write is sent through our server to Anthropic, the company whose AI model (Claude) writes the replies. Our server does not keep the text of your conversation.
  • Subscriptions are sold and billed by Apple or Google. We never see your card or your store account details.
  • Anonymous usage sharing is off unless you turn it on.
  • What you write goes to Anthropic in the United States, and our server's records are held by Cloudflare and may be processed outside British Columbia and Canada. See "Where your information goes".
  • On our side, only the developer can see what our server keeps.

What stays on your phone

The app saves these on your phone only:

  • your past conversations (up to the 200 most recent) and anything you mark about them, such as how sure you were before and after
  • your settings, such as which thinking partner you prefer and how long you like replies
  • which questions you have already seen, so they are not repeated too soon
  • the store's proof of your subscription, so you do not have to restore it every time
  • a random ID the app makes the first time it runs (see below)
  • your usage-sharing choice, and, once you have talked a question through, when you agreed that you are 18 or older and that what you write is sent to Anthropic
  • a log of what each reply cost us to make: when it was, which question, which AI model and reply length, and how much text went in and out
  • usage timings waiting to be sent, only while usage sharing is turned on (turning it off deletes them)

Deleting the app deletes all of this.

The random install ID

The first time it runs, the app makes up a random ID, something like dev_k3j9x.... It is not taken from your phone's hardware, your Apple or Google account, or any advertising ID, and it says nothing about who you are. Our server uses it to count each phone's conversations, to slow down misuse, and to tie together the records described below. If you delete and reinstall the app, it makes a new one.

When the app opens

Each time the app opens, and again after a purchase or a restore, it asks our server how many conversations you have left today and whether your subscription is active. It sends the random install ID, your phone's time-zone offset and, if you subscribe, the proof of purchase your app store gave the app. To answer, our server may ask Apple or Google whether the subscription is active (see "Subscriptions"). Nothing from this check is stored.

The first time you talk a question through

Reading questions needs no sign-up and no agreement. The first time you tap Think it over, a small sheet asks you to tick one box: "I'm 18 or older, and I agree that what I write is sent to Anthropic's AI, in the United States, so it can reply". It links to this policy and to our Terms of Use. A second box, "Share anonymous usage", is a separate choice: it starts unticked unless you have already turned usage sharing on in the settings, and you can leave it unticked. If you tap "Not now", nothing is sent and you can go on reading.

What happens when you talk a question through

Each time you send a message, the app sends our server:

  • the conversation so far and the message you just wrote
  • which question you are on and your reply settings
  • the random install ID
  • your phone's time-zone offset (for example "UTC minus 5 hours"), used once to decide when your day starts
  • if you subscribe, the proof of purchase your app store gave the app

Our server passes the conversation to Anthropic's API, gets the reply and sends it back to you. Before it does, it runs a simple automatic check on your message for two things only: signs that you may be in real distress (in which case it shows you where to find help instead), and attempts to rewrite the partner's instructions.

That check is done by the server on its own: no person reads your message. If it stopped a reply and you think it got it wrong, write to us. We will tell you why it happens, and you can tell us your side so we can take it into account.

Our server does not store the text of your conversations. The only text it keeps is a reply you choose to report, and the note you add to it, if any (see below).

Anthropic processes your conversation to write the reply. Anthropic's commercial terms say it may not train its models on content sent through its API by customers like us. Anthropic's published policy is that it deletes API inputs and outputs from its systems within 30 days, except where it needs to keep them longer to enforce its Usage Policy (for example, content its safety systems flag, which it says it may keep for up to 2 years) or where the law requires it. You can read Anthropic's own privacy information at https://privacy.claude.com.

Please do not put details in a conversation that you would not want to leave your phone, such as your full name, address or health details. The questions never need them.

Anonymous usage sharing (off unless you turn it on)

If you turn on Share anonymous usage, the app sends our server timing information: which screens you open and for how long, how long a question stays on screen and how far you scroll it, and for each message you send how long you spent reading and writing, how many characters it had and how many you deleted. It never includes anything you wrote. The server refuses any free text in these records. They carry the random install ID, never your name.

We use this to learn which questions people skip and which parts of the app work, so we can make it better. While the setting is off, the app sends none of this. You can turn it on or off at any time in the settings (the gear at the bottom of the question and conversation screens), whether or not you have ever talked a question through.

Reporting a reply

If a reply from the thinking partner is wrong, upsetting or inappropriate, you can report it from the conversation screen. The report sends our server the reply you reported, the reason you picked, and a note if you choose to add one, together with the details needed to look into it (which question it was, which conversation and which reply in it, and the random install ID). We keep reports for 180 days, use them only to look into the problem and improve the app, and then delete them.

Subscriptions

Subscriptions are sold by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play), and they handle all payment. When you subscribe, the store gives the app a proof of purchase. The app sends it to our server, and our server asks Apple or Google whether the subscription is active. We keep only a scrambled code made from the subscription's ID (a "salted hash": a one-way code that cannot be turned back into the ID). We use it to know that you are a subscriber and to apply the fair-use limits to the subscription rather than the phone. We never receive your name, email, card number or store account.

What we do not do

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no contact details.
  • No advertising and no advertising IDs.
  • No third-party analytics or tracking tools in the app.
  • No location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • We do not sell or rent information about you, and we do not share it with data brokers.
  • We do not track you across other companies' apps or websites.

Who can see it

Support Turtles is one person. Only the developer can open our server's records. No one else on our side has access.

Who else is involved

  • Anthropic writes the replies (see above).
  • Cloudflare hosts our server and its database.
  • Apple or Google sell the subscription and confirm it to our server.

They handle the information to provide their service to us, under their own terms and data protection commitments. We may also disclose information if the law requires it.

Where your information goes

We are in British Columbia, but the services we rely on are not. What you write goes to Anthropic, a company based in the United States. Our server runs on Cloudflare, a US company with data centres around the world, so the records described above may be processed outside Canada. Proof of purchase is checked with Apple or Google, both US companies.

Before relying on these companies, we looked at what each one receives, why, and how it is protected (encrypted connections, no names or contact details, as little as possible, and their written data protection terms). We will do that again before adding a new one.

Age

You must be 18 or older to talk a question through with the AI partner. The app asks you to confirm this the first time you tap "Think it over", before your first conversation. Reading the questions needs no confirmation, and the questions are rated for ages 13 and up, so younger people may read them.

Anyone can turn on anonymous usage sharing in the settings, without the age confirmation. It is off unless turned on and carries only the random install ID, never anything you write. Even so, if you are under 14, please do not turn it on without a parent's or guardian's consent. If you believe a child under 14 has turned it on, or has talked a question through, write to us and we will delete what we can find.

Your rights and choices

  • Usage sharing: off unless you turn it on, and you can switch it off at any time.
  • Withdrawing consent: you can stop at any time. Switch usage sharing off, stop talking questions through, or delete the app. Without the agreement on the first conversation, the app cannot send your words to Anthropic, so conversations stop working. Reading still works.
  • Everything on your phone: delete the app.
  • What our server holds: because there are no accounts, we cannot look you up by name or email. Our records are tied only to the random install ID and, for subscribers, the scrambled subscription code, and they are deleted on the schedule above.

To ask about what our server holds, write to supportturtles@duck.com with your install ID, which is shown at the bottom of the app's settings (the gear). You can:

  • ask what we hold about that ID, and get a copy in a structured, commonly used format (a JSON file)
  • ask us to correct it, or to delete it sooner
  • ask about the automatic check described above, and tell us your side

We reply within 30 days.

Complaints: write to us first and we will try to put it right. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the authority that oversees privacy where you live. In Canada, that is the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada (https://www.priv.gc.ca); in British Columbia, also the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for BC (https://www.oipc.bc.ca); in Québec, the Commission d'accès à l'information (https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca). Elsewhere, your local data protection authority.

How we look after your information

Because Support Turtles is one person, our rules are short:

  • The developer is responsible for all of it (see "Who is responsible" below).
  • Records are kept only as long as the table above says, and then deleted automatically.
  • Requests and complaints are answered by email within 30 days.
  • If something ever goes wrong, for example someone gets access to our records who should not, we write it down, limit the damage, and, if it could seriously harm people, report it to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and tell you. Since we have no way to contact you directly, we would do that in the app and on this page.
  • Before we change how the app handles information, or start sending it somewhere new, we look at the privacy risks first.

Security

The app talks to our server only over encrypted connections (HTTPS). Our server's access keys never leave the server. No system is perfectly secure, but we keep as little as we can, which is the best protection there is.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update the date at the top of this page. If a change matters, for example if we start collecting something new, we will say so in the app before it takes effect.

Who is responsible for your information

The person accountable for protecting personal information at Support Turtles is its owner, the developer.
