Think It Over: Age Suitability

Think It Over is a collection of philosophy thought experiments. Each one is a short piece of text, with a calm line drawing, that asks a question people have argued about for centuries: is it right to pull the lever, is a copied person the same person, can you trust a memory you cannot check. Reading them is free. A subscriber can also talk a question through with an AI thinking partner that asks about their reasons.

This page explains what the questions contain, so that a parent, teacher or reviewer can judge for themselves. A store rating is a set of yes or no answers, and it cannot show how a subject is treated.

How the content is presented

  • Text and quiet line drawings only. There are no photos, no video, no sound effects and no animation of anything happening to anyone.
  • Every question is a reasoning problem. Hard subjects appear because philosophy has always used them to test ideas: what we owe each other, what makes an act right or wrong, what a life is worth. They are put as questions to think about, never as scenes to watch.
  • Nothing is described graphically. A thought experiment may say that five people will die unless you act. It never shows how.
  • No advice of any kind. When a doctor, a patient or a treatment appears, it is a character in a moral question (should a doctor tell a patient the truth, who should get a scarce treatment). The app never gives medical, legal, financial or personal advice.

What is not in the app

  • No sexual content or nudity.
  • No swearing or crude humour.
  • No horror.
  • No real or simulated gambling, and no loot boxes.
  • No ads.
  • No contact with other people: no chat between users, no profiles, no sharing, nothing anyone writes is shown to anyone else.
  • No web browser.

The AI conversation

Talking a question through uses an AI model (Claude, made by Anthropic). Its replies stay on the question in the same way the questions do. Because it answers whatever a person writes, a reply may touch a subject the person raised. These safeguards apply:

  • The partner is instructed to stay on the question, never to give advice, and never to engage in romantic or sexual conversation.
  • Messages that suggest someone may be in real distress are not answered as philosophy. The app shows crisis line numbers instead.
  • Anthropic's own safety systems apply to every reply.
  • Any reply can be reported from the conversation screen, and we look into reports.

The conversation needs the user to confirm they are 18 or older. The first time someone taps "Think it over", the app asks them to confirm their age. That rule comes from the AI provider's usage policy, which treats anyone under 18 as a minor needing safeguards we have not built yet. It is not a sign that the content is adult. Reading the questions needs no confirmation.

For parents and teachers

The questions are suitable for thoughtful readers from about 13: the same subjects come up in secondary-school ethics, religious studies and philosophy classes. Younger readers may find some of the dilemmas upsetting, as they would in a book of moral philosophy. The AI conversation is for adults only, as explained above.
