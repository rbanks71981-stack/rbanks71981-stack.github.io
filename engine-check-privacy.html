ECEngine Check: OBD2 Reader

Privacy policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026

This policy describes what Engine Check: OBD2 Reader stores, what leaves your device, and who receives it. It covers the app published on Google Play under the package name com.obd2codereader.

Summary

The app is free to download, and reading and explaining fault codes is free. It is supported by advertising: a small banner ad at the bottom of its screens. A one-time in-app purchase, Engine Check Pro, unlocks clearing fault codes and removes all ads permanently.

The developer operates no server and receives no data about you, with one exception: an email you choose to write and send. Data does leave your device, to Google, and this policy says exactly what and why.

There is no account, no sign-in, and no analytics or crash-reporting service.

What the app reads from your car

Through the OBD2 adapter you supply and pair yourself, the app reads:

  • Stored and pending diagnostic trouble codes
  • Freeze-frame data the car recorded when a code was set
  • Readiness monitor status
  • Live sensor values — oxygen sensors, fuel trims, engine load, coolant temperature, road speed and similar

The app does not read your VIN and contains no code to request it. Nothing it reads from the vehicle is tied to an identifier for you or the car, and none of it is transmitted to the developer.

The app also builds a local database of fault-code descriptions. That is reference material shipped inside the app, not information about you.

Advertising

The free version shows ads served by Google AdMob:

  • a banner at the bottom of the app's screens, placed below the content and above the buttons. There are no full-screen ads.

To serve them, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and transmits:

  • Your device's advertising ID, a resettable identifier you control
  • Approximate location derived from your IP address
  • Device and app information, such as model, Android version and app version
  • Ad interaction data, such as whether an ad was shown or clicked

This is collected by Google, not by the developer, and is governed by Google's Privacy Policy and how Google uses data from apps that use its services. You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads.

Consent. Where the law requires it — the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and a growing list of US states — the app presents a consent form from Google's User Messaging Platform before any ad is requested, and honours your choice. If a consent control is required for you, an "Ad privacy options" entry appears in the app's menu so you can change or withdraw that choice at any time.

No ad is requested before consent is resolved.

The in-app purchase

A one-time purchase called Engine Check Pro unlocks clearing your car's fault codes and permanently stops the app showing advertising. It is processed entirely by Google Play Billing. Google handles the payment; the developer never sees your payment details, card number, name or billing address, and receives only anonymised sales totals from Google Play.

The app asks Google Play on each launch whether you own the purchase. It does not store that answer on your device. Because the purchase is tied to your Google account, it restores free of charge on a reinstall or a new phone.

Once the purchase is active, no ad is requested at all — not shown and not loaded — so the advertising SDK stops collecting the data described above.

What Android backs up to your Google Drive

Android's Auto Backup copies a limited set of app settings into a private folder of your own Google Drive, under your own Google account. Not the developer's, and not a shared one. The developer cannot see it or reach it.

Only these are included: your vehicle year/make/model, your repair notes, the adapter name and address, and the guide-completed flag.

Session logs and the fault-code database are deliberately excluded and are never copied off your device by this mechanism.

The backup is encrypted and does not count against your Drive storage. A backup can outlive an uninstall and be restored onto a new device. You can turn Android backup off for all apps in Settings → Google → Backup.

What leaves your device when you tap something

Each of these is a deliberate action, and each hands off to another app.

"Look up this code on the web" runs a Google search containing the fault code and the vehicle you entered — for example P0420 2015 Toyota Corolla trouble code.

"Shop car parts on Amazon" opens Amazon's car parts section. It sends no fault code, part name or vehicle details — the link is the same for everyone. It may carry an affiliate tag, so a purchase may earn the developer a commission at no extra cost to you. This is disclosed next to the button, and the link works whether or not you have bought Engine Check Pro.

"Submit feedback" opens your email app with a draft addressed to the developer containing the app version, your Android version and your phone model. Nothing is sent unless you send it. This is the only way the developer receives anything from you.

Sharing a session log sends the CSV or JSON wherever you choose to send it.

Once you are in your browser, email client or another app, that app's privacy policy applies rather than this one.

Permissions

Requested from you: Bluetooth connect (and legacy Bluetooth on Android 11 and below), used only to connect to the OBD2 adapter you have already paired. The app performs no Bluetooth scanning.

The app does not request location permission.

Declared automatically by Google's advertising and billing libraries, and never prompted for: internet and network state, the advertising ID permission, the Android Ad Services permissions, foreground service and wake lock.

Data retention and deletion

Everything the app stores is on your device. Uninstalling the app deletes all of it. An Android backup, if you have backup enabled, may persist until you delete it from your Google account.

To delete data Google holds from advertising, reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings and use Google's own controls. For purchase records, contact Google Play support.

The developer holds no database of users and so has nothing to delete on request, apart from any email you have sent, which you may ask to have deleted by writing to the address below.

Children

This app is not directed at children and is not designed for or targeted at users under 13.
